The feast of the Exaltation of the Holy Cross does not celebrate cruelty. It honours Christ, whose self-giving love met violence without allowing violence to have the last word. The cross is lifted before the Church because Jesus is lifted there: faithful, merciful and given for the life of the world.

On 14 September, the Rosary can help us look at the cross without sentimentality and without treating pain as holy in itself. We contemplate what God’s love does amid human sin, and we ask how that love should shape our care for people who suffer now.

## Understand what is being exalted

The [Liturgy Office readings for the feast](https://www.liturgyoffice.org.uk/Calendar/Sunday/Feasts.shtml) include the Son of Man being lifted up in John 3 and Christ’s self-emptying in Philippians 2. The feast directs attention towards salvation, humility and love, not admiration for an instrument of execution.

[Vatican News](https://www.vaticannews.va/en/liturgical-holidays/feast-of-the-exaltation-of-the-holy-cross.html) makes the same distinction in its account of the feast: the cruelty of the cross is not what Christians exalt. This matters because careless language about suffering can burden people who are already hurt.

Hold the crucifix of your Rosary for a moment. Ask not for an explanation of every pain, but for clearer sight of Christ’s love and the courage to oppose what wounds human dignity.

## Pray the Sorrowful Mysteries truthfully

The Sorrowful Mysteries trace Christ’s Passion through fear, violence, mockery, exhaustion and death. Pray them without making the scenes more graphic than the Gospels require. Their power lies not in emotional intensity but in the person of Jesus, who remains faithful to the Father and loving towards others.

At Gethsemane, pray for people facing dread and for those who stay awake beside them. At the scourging and crowning with thorns, pray for victims of torture, humiliation and public contempt. As Jesus carries the cross, remember those carrying responsibilities beyond their strength. At the Crucifixion, hold the dying, the bereaved and all who accompany them.

Do not rush from suffering to a neat lesson. Let lament be part of Christian prayer.

## Never use the cross to excuse abuse

No one should be told to endure violence, coercion or degrading treatment because it is “their cross.” Jesus’ Passion does not give an abuser permission to harm, nor does it require a victim to remain in danger. Christian self-giving is freely offered love; abuse takes freedom away.

If you or someone else is unsafe, seek help through emergency services, safeguarding professionals, a domestic abuse service or another qualified local source. A priest or parish safeguarding representative may assist spiritually and practically, but urgent safety needs should be handled by those equipped to respond.

Forgiveness, where it becomes possible, does not cancel boundaries, justice or accountability. The cross exposes sin and reveals the worth of the person who has been harmed.

## See love as active, not passive

Christ’s love on the cross is not indifference. It gives, forgives, entrusts, speaks truth and completes his mission. When the feast shapes daily life, it should lead towards active care rather than admiration of endurance for its own sake.

Ask where love needs a practical form: staying with someone at an appointment, challenging demeaning speech, making space for grief, giving to relief work or accepting help you cannot provide alone. Some acts will involve sacrifice, but sacrifice should serve life and dignity.

Discernment matters. Not every demand is a call from God, and not every refusal is selfish. Wise love can say yes, no, wait or seek help.

## Let dawn speak of Easter

The cross is never separated from the Resurrection. Strong dawn light on wood does not erase the wounds; it shows that death does not possess the whole horizon. Christian hope honours what happened while trusting that God’s life reaches beyond it.

This protects the Rosary from becoming trapped in sorrow. Even while praying the Passion, we pray as people who know the tomb will not hold Christ. Hope is not denial. It is confidence placed in God while the present remains hard.

If hope feels remote, let the Church’s prayer carry it for you. You do not need to manufacture an emotion that is not there.

The dawn image also suggests a patient kind of courage: keeping vigil, asking for help and refusing to let despair name the whole future.

## Lift up love in one concrete act

The feast asks more than a glance at a cross on the wall. It asks whether Christ’s pattern of merciful, truthful and self-giving love will be visible in us. Beadlight can guide the sequence of the Sorrowful Mysteries, while the crucifix in your hand keeps their centre before you.

Pray the five Sorrowful Mysteries today, holding one person or group affected by violence in each. When the Rosary ends, choose one action that protects dignity: make a safe check-in, support a trusted service, report a concern through the proper route or apologise for words that caused harm. Let love be lifted high through what you do.
